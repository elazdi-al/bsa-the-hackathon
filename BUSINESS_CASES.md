# Swissquote wallet business cases

This tracker extracts the commercial ideas from the supplied Track 2 brief and project notes, then develops them into offers and validation questions. It excludes the engineering plan and legal analysis.

All cases are proposals. Priority is a working order for investigation, not an approved roadmap. No pricing, market demand, cost savings, or production readiness has been validated here. References in the source to current Swissquote products and fees have not been independently checked.

## Core business case

Clients who want self-custody should be able to keep using Swissquote for buying, selling, transfers, and support. Swissquote earns revenue from services clients choose to use around their wallet. A simpler experience may also attract clients who find existing self-custody wallets difficult to manage.

Success means retaining an active client relationship and earning incremental revenue. Self-custodied balances should not automatically count as bank deposits or assets under management. Clients must remain free to move their assets elsewhere.

## Offers to develop

### BC-01. Keep self-custody clients connected to Swissquote

The source proposes a wallet inside the Swissquote experience, close to the client's existing bank account.

Start with existing clients who withdraw crypto to external wallets. Offer a convenient way to manage their wallet and return to Swissquote when they want to trade or move money between accounts. Extend acquisition efforts to new clients once existing-client demand is demonstrated.

Validate with client interviews and a pilot. Compare funded wallet activation, 90-day client retention, and subsequent Swissquote trading activity against a comparable group. Deduct revenue displaced from existing Swissquote services when estimating the benefit.

### BC-02. Charge for transactions and swaps

The source proposes fees by transaction type and a percentage fee on swaps.

Test a clear Swissquote service fee for swaps, with the total expected cost shown before approval. Leave basic transfers between Swissquote-linked wallets free of Swissquote transfer fees, as proposed in the source. Define which other transactions justify a paid service before charging for them.

Measure completed swaps, repeat usage, fee sensitivity, and contribution per swap after service costs. Volume alone is insufficient if incentives or sponsorship consume the fee revenue.

### BC-03. Offer a premium subscription

The source proposes a subscription as an alternative to transaction fees, including swaps without Swissquote fees.

Test an optional plan for frequent users. A candidate package includes waived Swissquote swap fees within a stated allowance, a larger sponsored transaction allowance, and priority support. Compare this with paying per swap before choosing a model.

Describe the benefit as a waived Swissquote fee. Liquidity-provider fees, spreads, price impact, and network costs may still apply. Basic access and recovery should remain available without a premium subscription.

Validate willingness to pay, subscription retention, and margin among frequent users. Account for both the costs premium users generate and the transaction fees they would otherwise have paid.

### BC-04. Make transfers easier to start and repeat

The source proposes gasless transactions and free transfers between Swissquote wallets.

Offer a defined allowance of sponsored transfers so clients can send supported assets without first buying a separate network token. State what Swissquote covers and what happens when the allowance runs out. Sponsorship is an acquisition and usage expense with a budget.

Compare first-transfer completion and repeat usage with the cost per active client. Test whether the additional paid activity or retention covers sponsorship costs. Include abuse and unusually high network fees in the cost estimate.

### BC-05. Reduce the burden of wallet support

The source identifies lower operating costs as a business benefit and proposes assisted recovery, account linking, and wallet-control verification.

Turn these into guided client journeys that reduce manual handling of setup, transfer, and lost-device questions. Recovery also addresses a reason clients avoid self-custody in the first place.

Measure support contacts per active wallet, staff time per case, successful recovery completion, and total cost to serve. Compare with a baseline that includes the additional support obligations the new wallet creates. Automated checks only produce savings when they actually replace manual work.

### BC-06. Offer understandable access to dApps

The source proposes approved dApps, transaction previews, risk checks, and extra checks for large transactions.

Package these as assisted access for clients who want to use on-chain services but struggle to assess transactions. Begin with a small set of supported activities tied to a clear client need. Revenue could come through the swap service or premium plan; avoid assuming another fee is necessary.

Measure completed activities, repeat use, related fee revenue, and support incidents. Screening and approval should not be marketed as a guarantee against loss. Count revenue under the service that earns it, rather than counting it again here.

### BC-07. Add staking services

The source proposes staking rewards and a potentially lower service fee through self-custody.

Explore an optional staking service with a disclosed fee and clear withdrawal conditions. Test whether clients value convenience enough to pay for it. Treat lower operating costs from self-custody as an assumption to measure, not a reason to promise a lower fee today.

Track participation, net service revenue, support costs, and retention. Present variable rewards and product-specific risks accurately; do not promise a fixed return. Production feasibility needs a separate assessment.

### BC-08. Connect payments to a card and rewards

The source proposes a wallet-linked Swissquote debit card, loyalty points, and cashback for online crypto payments.

Evaluate these together as a payments offer. Identify when crypto converts into the payment currency and disclose any conversion fee. Fund rewards from an explicit marketing budget or the payment revenue available to Swissquote under partner agreements.

Test net contribution per active card after processing, conversion, rewards, fraud, and support costs. Compare rewarded clients with a control group to distinguish repeat usage from short-lived incentive spending.

### BC-09. Sell services to business and wealth clients

The source names business and wealth-management wallet services without defining the offer.

Develop two separate customer interviews. Businesses may pay for payment administration and reporting. Wealth clients may value consolidated reporting and access to a relationship manager. These are proposed extensions of the source idea, not established customer requirements.

Seek paid pilot interest before building either offer. Measure willingness to pay against onboarding effort and ongoing servicing costs. A general claim that professional clients will pay more is insufficient.

### BC-10. Explore collateralized lending

The source proposes borrowing from Swissquote to trade crypto and an on-chain collateral vault.

Define the client need first, including whether clients want liquidity without selling their holdings. Then assess a limited collateralized lending product with explicit borrowing costs and collateral terms. The vault is an implementation option, not the business case itself.

Assess demand and net financing income after funding costs, credit losses, liquidation costs, operations, and any applicable capital costs. Price volatility and concentration can erase gross interest revenue. Keep this outside the initial offer until commercial, risk, and legal feasibility have been assessed.

## Initial packaging hypothesis

| Offer | Proposed contents | Economic purpose |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Basic wallet | Wallet access, recovery, no Swissquote fee for transfers between linked wallets, bounded sponsorship | Acquire and retain active clients |
| Pay per use | Disclosed Swissquote swap fee | Earn revenue when clients trade |
| Optional premium | Swap-fee allowance, additional sponsorship, priority support | Test recurring revenue for frequent users |
| Future services | Staking, card payments, professional services, lending | Add only after demand and economics are validated |

No price or allowance is set yet. The first comparison should be pay-per-use versus optional premium, using observed client activity and actual service costs.

## Economics to track

Calculate monthly contribution as Swissquote service revenue minus sponsored network fees, partner fees, rewards, screening, infrastructure, support, and attributable losses. Track development and other fixed costs separately when assessing break-even.

Track retention benefits separately from direct wallet revenue. Count only incremental retained revenue, subtract displaced existing revenue, and avoid counting the same swap or card income in several business cases.

## Next iteration

- Interview existing clients who use external wallets. Record why they left and what would bring their activity back.
- Measure the actual cost of a sponsored transfer, a swap, and a recovery support case.
- Test fee and subscription packages with occasional and frequent users separately.
- Choose one initial paid service based on demand and contribution, then revise the proposed order above.

For each update, keep the case ID and record the evidence, decision, owner, and next test. Use statuses `Hypothesis`, `Testing`, `Validated`, `Deferred`, or `Rejected`. A working demo alone does not validate a business case.
