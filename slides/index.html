Our answer: the wallet belongs to the client, and Swissquote is linked to it, not in charge of it. Only the client's Face ID can move funds. The wallet works on its own, needs no Swissquote account, and keeps working if the client leaves Swissquote, so there is no lock-in. And instead of twelve words on paper, a lost phone is recovered with a Recovery Key file kept at home and two independent helpers, Swissquote and an independent service. The demo follows one client through four steps: create, pay, lose the phone, recover it. Then come the scenarios that show why the design holds, the law, and the business model.

One idea carries the design. The wallet key is split 2-of-3 with Shamir secret sharing: Share A on the phone, sealed so only Face ID opens it, B at Swissquote, C at an independent service. Any two rebuild the key on the phone, which keeps it in memory for three hours at most. The key alone moves nothing: the smart account also checks a passkey signature on every payment, so Swissquote and the independent service together still cannot move funds. Share C also has a second job, exit mode: if Swissquote is gone, it lets the client leave without it. We come back to that at the end of the story.

Step two, pay. A payment needs only two things: the client's face and one more share. Face ID unseals Share A on the phone and signs the payment with the passkey; Swissquote releases Share B after that Face ID, and A and B rebuild the wallet key, which signs too. The smart account verifies both signatures on chain, so a payment without the client's face is rejected by the chain itself, not by our server. The rebuilt key stays in the phone's memory for three hours, so the next payments skip Share B, but every payment still needs a fresh Face ID. Swissquote screens and submits the payment, and for a linked wallet it also pays the fee. An unlinked wallet pays its own in ETH.

Linking is optional and comes later, from Settings. To use financial features in line with Swiss law, the client links the wallet to their Swissquote account: the app redirects to Swissquote, the client is already a known, identified client there, and Swissquote accepts. Face ID then signs that this wallet belongs to their client number, with the passkey every payment needs, so no key leaves the phone and nothing goes on chain. No ID is scanned or checked. That signed statement is Swissquote's Travel Rule ownership proof, so money moves between the account and the wallet without extra checks. A linked wallet also gets its network fees paid, and access to Plus, swaps and rewards. Without a link the wallet still works: it pays its own fees in ETH, and transfers from the Swissquote account take its usual procedure for external wallets.

Last, the brief asks what happens if Swissquote's services are gone for good. After 30 days without Swissquote, the independent service opens exit mode: when the client's passkey approves a payment, it releases Share C for that payment. Two scenarios show why this model beats the alternatives. First, the worst case for a bank wallet: Swissquote and the independent service collude. Together they hold Shares B and C, enough to rebuild the wallet key. In a 2-of-3 MPC wallet, that would be enough to sign. Here it isn't: the smart account demands a passkey signature on every payment, and the passkey sits behind the client's Face ID on their phone. So the chain itself refuses. Neither the bank nor its partner, alone or together, can spend.

Architecture.

Solid parts run for real in the demo. Yellow dashed parts are mocked.

Four parties and a file at home. The phone holds the passkey and Share A and rebuilds the wallet key in memory for up to three hours. Swissquote screens every payment, submits every operation, pays the gas for linked wallets, and holds Share B. The independent service is a real separate process with its own database, holding Share C, which the phone sends it directly; only the company behind it is a stand-in. The Recovery Key file stays at home: it starts or cancels a recovery and locks a lost phone. The chain enforces the passkey and the 48-hour delay; its EntryPoint is the standard contract that checks and runs every account operation. In exit mode, with Swissquote gone, the phone submits operations itself and pays its own gas. Yellow parts are mocked: the Swissquote sign-in behind the optional account link, the sanctions and simulation data, the secure chip, and the vaults, which seal shares in software here and would be HSMs in production.

Assistance, not custody. Ownership signed when you link.

Our reading of Swiss law, for counsel to confirm.

Custody
Swissquote can’t move funds.

Not even with the independent service. Withholding help or cancelling a recovery is assistance.

Travel Rule
Ownership signed with Face ID.

Linking signs the wallet to the client number: proof for FINMA. Unlinked wallets take the usual check.

Sending institution
The client sends, not Swissquote.

The client signs, the account sends. Swissquote screens and submits, never holds or orders funds.

Rules from
Custody turns on who can move the funds: Swissquote can't, alone or with the independent service; it can only withhold help or cancel a recovery, which we argue is assistance, still under AML rules, hence the screening. For the Travel Rule, linking is optional. When the client links their Swissquote account, they sign with Face ID, the passkey every payment needs, that the wallet belongs to their client number, and Swissquote keeps that statement, so ownership evidence exists without a test transfer and money moves between the account and the wallet without extra checks. An unlinked wallet takes Swissquote's usual procedure for external wallets. When the client pays a third party, the client signs and the account sends, so we argue Swissquote is not the sending institution, an open question for counsel. Swissquote pays the gas only for wallets it can tie to an identified client, so an unlinked wallet pays its own.

How Swissquote makes money. It keeps all of the Plus subscription. It earns the published 1 percent on swaps, half that for Plus members, who swap more. The production path is the native Swissquote app with the secure chip, real HSMs, a contracted independent service and Base mainnet.
