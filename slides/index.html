Fuse is a wallet concept Swissquote could offer its clients. We focus on two flows: approving a payment and recovering a lost phone. Shamir sharing distributes the wallet key; the smart account supplies the additional user authorization. Keep the distinction between a share and a separate authorization key clear throughout.

There are three shares of one secp256k1 Wallet Key. The passkey and Recovery Key are separate credentials. During onboarding, Share C goes from the phone directly to a partner URL pinned in the app build. Swissquote does not receive C in that flow. A alone, B alone, or C alone cannot reconstruct the Wallet Key.

02 · The Shamir mechanism

Two points recover the secret.

A random line hides the key at its intercept: f(x) = k + a·x mod n

Any valid pair

A + BA + CB + C

Interpolation reconstructs the same Wallet Key.

Verify the split

Feldman commitments let each holder check its share before storing it.

In this build, the full key is reconstructed on the phone, then used to sign.

The real calculation uses the secp256k1 group order, not floating-point arithmetic or an ordinary plotted line. The graph is a schematic. Shares occupy positions 1, 2, and 3. Any two distinct valid shares reconstruct f(0), the key. Commitments kG and aG allow a holder to check yG = kG + x·aG. This is Shamir secret sharing followed by ECDSA signing, not distributed threshold ECDSA that keeps the full key from ever existing on one device.

The smart contract does not see Shamir shares or know which pair was used. It verifies the Wallet Key's ECDSA signature and the second authorization. Normal mode requires WebAuthn. Recovery mode requires the offline Recovery Key signature and is restricted to a direct initiateRecovery call. Both signatures cover the UserOperation hash, which binds the proposed action.

Share registration is checked against commitments. Swissquote issues a signed ticket that the partner validates before accepting C. The newly generated Wallet Key then signs the Recovery Key binding, which both holders verify. The account is registered with its signer, passkey, Recovery Key address, and guardian. The factory predicts its address before deployment. The Recovery Key file contains a secret, not merely an account identifier.

The key cache lasts at most 3 hours. Each payment still needs a fresh passkey assertion.

On a cache miss, Swissquote verifies the passkey assertion against the prepared normal operation before releasing B. The phone reconstructs the key locally from A and B and checks its signer address. A cache hit skips that reconstruction but still prompts for passkey approval. Swissquote's current backend submits through EntryPoint itself and pays via the paymaster; it does not require an external bundler. Refused assistance does not freeze the account on-chain.

The client still authorizes its use.

Even colluding holders of B and C lack the second signature.

To send funds

The registered passkey must also sign.

Held by the client

To start recovery

The separate Recovery Key must also sign.

Saved by the client at home

Services can refuse assistance. Swissquote's guardian can cancel recovery. These affect availability.

Avoid equating no spending authority with no power at all. Swissquote can withhold B, sponsorship, and submission, and can cancel recoveries as guardian. The partner can withhold C. A malicious client app or a compromised usable passkey is also outside this collusion claim. The contract's separate authorization checks are what prevent B+C alone from spending or initiating recovery.

07 · Lost-phone recovery

A new device. New credentials.
Authorization from the old key.

The Recovery Key approves exactly which signer and passkey will replace the old ones.

Before the request

The new phone creates a passkey and fresh shares A′, B′, C′, bound to the same Recovery Key.

The client loads the saved Recovery Key. The new phone creates its replacement passkey and Wallet Key first. Swissquote prepares the recovery. The client signs its exact hash with the Recovery Key. Swissquote releases old B and issues a five-minute approval ticket. The partner verifies the ticket, recomputed operation hash, wallet, recovery-only call, and bound Recovery Key signature before releasing old C. Only the new phone receives B and C together in this flow and reconstructs the old key for one recovery signature. The identity step is mocked in the current prototype.

Cancel through the assisted recovery path.

Old credentials remain active until finalization. Cancellation is possible while recovery is pending.

RECOVERY_DELAY is a fixed 48-hour constant. initiateRecovery records block.timestamp plus that delay. An additional initiation is rejected while one is pending. The old phone can cancel with a normal operation. Swissquote can cancel as guardian; its app endpoint requires a Recovery Key-signed cancellation request. Cancellation is still permitted after the deadline if finalization has not happened. This is not a spending freeze: the old credentials can still authorize payments while pending.

Anyone may finalize after the deadline, but they cannot change the replacement chosen by the signed recovery operation. finalizeRecovery installs the recorded signer and passkey and clears the pending recovery. The address, Recovery Key, and guardian remain unchanged. No token transfer is performed by recovery. Normal payments during the wait can still change balances. The recovered phone then uses its new A and Swissquote's new B for normal signing.

Be precise about what the demonstration proves. Identity verification accepts nonempty mock document and selfie strings. Server shares are encrypted in SQLite with environment-provided keys, not HSMs. Share A uses AES-GCM with WebAuthn PRF where supported, but falls back to unsealed storage without PRF. The key cache is memory-only with a three-hour expiry; the current browser code has no explicit pagehide wipe hook. Production alerts and real bank integration are not implemented. The repository supports local demonstration and Base Sepolia configuration; the contract rules are the same regardless of the configured network.

The takeaway is the separation of key reconstruction and permission to use it. A normal payment requires the Wallet Key and passkey. Recovery requires the old Wallet Key and the offline Recovery Key, then an on-chain waiting period before replacing credentials. The client's wallet address is preserved. Swissquote helps without gaining ordinary spending authority, while retaining practical assistance and guardian powers.
